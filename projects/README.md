## About

Not every interesting idea needs to remain an experiment.

Some discoveries might justify building a tool, application or more substantial piece of software. Others might begin with a practical engineering problem rather than a machine-learning investigation.

This is where those projects live.

The intention is to build useful, understandable and maintainable software without creating unnecessary infrastructure or abstractions.

Projects should be independently understandable, with their own documentation, dependencies, testing and structure appropriate to their complexity.

They don't need to share a common framework or even use machine learning if another approach solves the problem more effectively.

**Build what the problem requires, not what the technology makes fashionable.**

## Engineering Approach

- **Solve a real problem.** Useful software starts with a reason to exist.
- **Keep the design proportionate.** Complexity should earn its place.
- **Understand the implementation.** Libraries and abstractions are tools, not substitutes for engineering judgement.
- **Test appropriately.** Confidence should come from evidence, not assumptions.
- **Document limitations.** Be clear about what's implemented, experimental or unfinished.
- **Allow projects to evolve.** Early designs aren't sacred, and not every prototype deserves a permanent home.

A project may begin as a small utility and develop into something more substantial.

Equally, a useful tool might remain fifty lines of Python.

Both are perfectly valid outcomes.
